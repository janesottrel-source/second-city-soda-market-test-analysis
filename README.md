# Second-City-Soda-Market-Testing-and-Scale-Up-Analysis-for-Volt-Rush-Energy-Line
Consulting-style analytics project for Second City Soda's Volt Rush energy drink. Uses Excel, survey analysis, multiple regression, ANOVA, t-tests, forecasting and financial modeling on an 8-week market test to recommend what to scale, where to launch, and how to promote it.

## Overview
Second City Soda (SCS), a mid-sized soft drink manufacturer based in Chicago, is entering the energy drink market. It ran an 8-week sales market test of three products (Volt Rush 12 oz, Volt Rush Zero Sugar 12 oz, and Volt Rush 16 oz) across 19 Chicago neighborhoods.

This project acts as an analytics consultant for SCS. It answers three questions the company has about scaling regionally:

- **Where** should it expand first?
- **What** product should it lead with?
- **How** should it promote and market the product?

## Recommendation
- **What:** Lead with the 16 oz can. It drove 37% of unit sales, sold the most units, and had the highest share of sales with no promotion (42%).
- **Where:** Target dense neighborhoods in big Midwest cities. Near North Side, Loop and Lake View led the test.
- **How:** Use occasional BOGO 50% promotions. Aim marketing at working city adults ages 18–49, especially coffee drinkers, and reach both men and women.

## Key Findings
- **Regression:** The 16 oz format added about 291 units. BOGO and local events also lifted sales. Competition and higher prices reduced sales.
- **Hypothesis tests:** The 16 oz sold about 10% more units than the 12 oz Regular and about 25% more than the 12 oz Zero Sugar (99.9%+ confidence).
- **Survey:** Purchase intent was strongest among adults 18–49 and coffee drinkers. It was nearly identical between men and women, and weaker among adults 50+.
- **Financials:** The test earned $20,700 in profit on $445,071 in revenue (5% net margin, 44% contribution margin). Fixed and distribution costs held profit down, so scaling one best-selling product should lower variable costs.

## Tools
- **Microsoft Excel:** data cleaning, analysis, statistical testing, financial modeling and charts
- **Microsoft PowerPoint:** final presentation for stakeholders

## Methods
- **Descriptive analysis:** unit sales by product, neighborhood and promotion; survey purchase intent by age, gender and current drink
- **Multiple linear regression:** units sold modeled on unit price and categorical (dummy) variables for product, promotion, competition level and local events, with baselines and statistical significance
- **Hypothesis testing:** single-factor ANOVA, plus one-tailed t-tests comparing 16 oz sales to each 12 oz product
- **Time-series forecasting:** linear trend forecast of weekly 16 oz sales with confidence bounds
- **Financial analysis:** revenue, variable and fixed costs, profit, net and contribution margins, and scale-up scenarios for the 16 oz product
